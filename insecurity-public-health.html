Insecurity as a Public Health Issue

Understanding the hidden health burden of violence and instability

Dr. Chidinma Ndubuisi · May 2025 · 3 min read
Featured Image

Insecurity is often discussed in political or security terms, but its impact on public health is significant and far-reaching. Beyond immediate physical harm, insecurity disrupts healthcare access, weakens health systems, and increases the burden of both communicable and non-communicable diseases.

Populations exposed to chronic insecurity experience higher rates of trauma, anxiety, and depression. Maternal and child health outcomes are also negatively affected due to reduced access to antenatal care, immunization services, and emergency healthcare.

From a public health perspective, insecurity is not just a security challenge — it is a structural health determinant that shapes population wellbeing over time.
